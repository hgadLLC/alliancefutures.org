  On 27 October, Ambassador **Lyu Jin** presented Naoero&rsquo;s first Chinese Ambassador Awards alongside Education Minister **Asterio Appi**.

  Students received cash in red envelopes. The embassy paid **AUD 37,200** (about US$25,700) to 198 students across five schools, from AUD 100 per primary-school student to AUD 600 for the top performers at Naoero Secondary. It also handed the Department of Education a second batch of 30 computers, following an earlier 30 computers and 600 schoolbags.

  - title: Deep Sea Engagement in Naoero
    body: |
      On 26 October, the research vessel ***Shenhai Yihao*** (Deep Sea No. 1), carrying the crewed submersible ***Jiaolong***, docked at Aiwo on the second leg of China&rsquo;s **Ocean 100 expedition**. The ship&rsquo;s crew and Ambassador **Lyu Jin** led President **David Adeang**, Speaker **Marcus Stephen**, and Chief Justice **Jay Udit** on a tour and showed them samples &ldquo;collected from the waters around Naoero.&rdquo; More than 150 dei-Naoero attended a public open day. The embassy said attendees showed &ldquo;appreciation for Chinese technology&rdquo; and highlighted *Jiaolong*&rsquo;s first female pilot to the young women in the crowd.

      [Naoeroan media](https://www.facebook.com/share/v/1A8RMKUL7x/), not the embassy, shared parts of the speech in which Lyu highlighted the expedition&rsquo;s purpose and China&rsquo;s aims for deep-sea cooperation. He said the expedition would give &ldquo;Naoero a clearer picture of its marine resources&rdquo; to help the country &ldquo;better manage, protect, and sustainably develop its marine resources, diversify its economy, and support long term growth.&rdquo;

      [*Xinhua*](https://www.news.cn/world/20261027/3412a32278ce40c888dbf1bd76008b3d/c.html) reported that Lyu urged cooperation in &ldquo;marine spatial planning, marine ecological monitoring, marine industry development, and marine talent training&rdquo; and that the two sides met to define next steps. The Ambassador and the ship&rsquo;s chief scientist presented President Adeang with a model of the submersible. Adeang is Naoero&rsquo;s leading advocate for seabed mining in the **Clarion&ndash;Clipperton Zone**, where the country sponsors NORI&rsquo;s exploration contract, so the gift suited a known interest.

      *Jiaolong* arrived with **AI upgrades that carry potential military uses**. As the *Shenhai Yihao* reached Naoero, *Xinhua* reported that the submersible had tested two new AI-powered systems on trial dives in the Philippine Basin. The first technology is a fault-diagnosis and decision-support system that helps identify mechanical problems. The [*South China Morning Post*&rsquo;s](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3372528/china-tests-powerful-ai-could-one-day-be-used-submarines) (SCMP) reported that this system could have military applications in nuclear-powered submarines. The second system is an AI imagery-analysis program that identifies objects on the seabed. It can reconstruct the submersible&rsquo;s diving trajectory, analyze marine species, and generate reports on seabed ecosystems. *SCMP* reported that a system of this kind could be adapted to acoustic data to support military sonar. The vessel that offered Naoero a clearer picture of its marine resources had tested systems that *SCMP* says could serve submarine and sonar programs.

  - title: Kiribati Engages China on Ocean Resources
    body: |
      From 14 to 23 October, a delegation led by Kiribati Minister for Fisheries and Ocean Resources (MFOR) **Ribanataake Tiwau** held **10 meetings** across Shanghai, Zhoushan, and Beijing. Four of these events were board meetings for Kiribati-China joint ventures&mdash;Kiribati Fish Limited, Kiribati Island Fish Limited, Kiribati Tuna Fishing Company Limited, Kiribati Blue Pacific. A fifth meeting involved **China National Fisheries Company (CNFC)**. MFOR reported about US$2.6 million in dividends from the joint ventures to the Kiribati government and said the ventures buy more than US$28 million in fishing days each year. Together, the two figures equal **more than 10 percent of the government&rsquo;s [2024](https://policycommons.net/artifacts/52056007/government-of-kiribati-annual-account-report-2024/52954629/) revenue**.

      On 20 October, Tiwau signed a tripartite joint venture framework agreement with CNFC and a Fiji-based partner. The general manager of China National Agriculture Development Group, the state-owned enterprise that operates CNFC, said that the cooperation &ldquo;covers purse-seine and longline fishing operations, as well as seafood processing and exports.&rdquo;

      The delegation met the Vice President from **China MinMetals Corporation**, the Director General of the **China Deep Ocean Affairs Administration**, and the General Manager of the **China Mechanical and Engineering Company (CMEC)**. The Ministry did not describe these meetings, but the other events and the readout offer clues to their purpose. The CNFC agreement emphasizes building Kiribati&rsquo;s domestic processing industry, which may explain the CMEC meeting. The readout reported agreed next steps in development areas that include deep-sea minerals and thanked, among others, the chairman of **Marawa**, the Kiribati state-owned company that holds an International Seabed Authority contract in the Clarion&ndash;Clipperton Zone. Together, these references make it likely that the Minmetals and deep ocean administration meetings concerned seabed minerals.

      At the end of the trip, Tiwau and his delegation hosted a banquet in Beijing for i-Kiribati students studying in China. After returning, he was among the first officials to receive Ambassador **Yao Fei**.
