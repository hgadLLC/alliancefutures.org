  - title: Building Resilient Support for Beijing&rsquo;s Policies
    body: |
      On 15 August, Ambassador **Lyu Jin** met with Naoeroan parliamentarians following their return from China&rsquo;s late-July Seminar for Pacific Island Countries&rsquo; Parliamentarians. The group included Timothy Ika, Jason Bingham Agir, and Pyon Deiye; Deputy Minister for Health and Medical Services Wanganeen Emiu joined the meeting.

      Lyu used the meeting to brief the MPs on Beijing&rsquo;s **Law on Promoting Ethnic Unity and Progress**, a subject the seminar had not covered. The new law codifies China&rsquo;s assimilationist ethnic policy and authorizes extraterritorial enforcement against anyone found to disturb China&rsquo;s &ldquo;ethnic unity.&rdquo; According to the Chinese-language embassy readout, the MPs praised China&rsquo;s social governance and said Naoero should draw lessons from it.

      The briefing reflects a core diplomatic objective for Beijing: building international support for domestic policies that draw international criticism. The one-China principle dominates China&rsquo;s Pacific diplomatic agenda, but support for China&rsquo;s ethnic policies&mdash;including its approach to Tibet and Xinjiang&mdash;also matters to the CCP.

      A separate engagement in Port Vila illustrates how Beijing builds durability into that support. Ambassador **Li Minggang** hosted a party-to-party exchange with Vanuatu&rsquo;s **Land and Justice Party**&mdash;the second such meeting since July. The party reaffirmed its commitment to Beijing&rsquo;s one-China principle. China&rsquo;s diplomats collect these reaffirmations from sitting governments, but cultivating political parties and parliamentarians outside government ensures the relationship survives when administrations change. The week&rsquo;s two engagements&mdash;Naoeroan MPs and a Vanuatuan opposition party&mdash;follow the same logic.

  - title: China Tours Temotu
    body: |
      Earlier this month, Counsellor **Li Qinghua** traveled to **Temotu Province**&mdash;the Solomon Islands&rsquo; easternmost island group&mdash;alongside officials from the Ministry of Rural Development. Previous Chinese embassy engagement with Temotu Province has been limited to meetings with provincial officials in Honiara and donations to its constituencies; **Li&rsquo;s visit marked the first time a Chinese diplomatic representative traveled to the province itself.** The delegation met with Provincial Premier Stanley Tehiahua and his executive to discuss infrastructure, fisheries, renewable energy, and technical training. Li attended a Rural Sustainable Development Program handover in Temotu Nende Constituency&mdash;21 boats and outboard motors&mdash;and the delegation toured Chinese construction projects, including Solomon Islands National Broadband Infrastructure Project towers and the Lata airport upgrade.

      The Chinese embassy funded roughly **US$12,450** for two boats and an engine for Small Malaita Constituency under the Small and Sustainable Development Project Fund. In East Honiara, the embassy handed over 400 solar street lights; MP and Health Minister Morris To&rsquo;iraena described China as a &ldquo;true friend and development partner.&rdquo; In Burns Creek, the **Police Liaison Team** and the Royal Solomon Islands Police Force jointly delivered two embassy-funded water tanks to the community&mdash;the CPLT&rsquo;s involvement in a sanitation project continuing a pattern of China&rsquo;s security personnel operating across development and policing lines.
